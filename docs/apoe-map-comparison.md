# APOE ε4 across disease maps

**Source-checked draft · apoe-e4-tsuang-2013-0.1.0 · November 28, 2026**

Tsuang and colleagues used a dominant carrier model, grouping people with one or two APOE ε4 alleles against people with none. Multinomial logistic regression adjusted for sex and age at death. The study used the same 269 autopsied controls for both case-group estimates. [Original author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3580799/)

| Study group | Participants | ε4 carriers | Adjusted odds ratio | 95% CI |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Alzheimer’s disease (AD) | 244 | 153 | 9.9 | 6.4–15.3 |
| DLB with low or absent Alzheimer’s pathology (pDLB) | 91 | 47 | 6.1 | 3.5–10.5 |
| Shared controls | 269 | 37 | | |

Carrier counts sum the ε2/ε4, ε3/ε4 and ε4/ε4 genotype counts in Table 2. The two estimates and intervals come from the adjusted columns of Table 3. The AD group had high Alzheimer’s changes (Braak IV–VI with moderate or frequent CERAD plaques) and no detected Lewy body changes. The pDLB group had dementia with limbic or neocortical Lewy body changes and lower levels of Alzheimer’s changes. The authors grouped everything below the high threshold as low. The paper also reports separate Parkinson disease dementia and mixed high Alzheimer’s plus Lewy body pathology groups. [Classification, Table 2, and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3580799/)

The controls were cognitively normal at entry and unimpaired at their last assessment within three years of death. Their autopsies had no detected Lewy body changes or high Alzheimer’s changes. The US study enrolled self-identified White participants and lacked ancestry-informative genetic markers. Its retrospective case selection and classification limit generalization. Interpret each odds ratio against the controls as defined in the study. A direct comparison between the case groups would require a separate estimate. [Methods and discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3580799/)
